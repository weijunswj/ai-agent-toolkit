# Toolkit Controller: design principles
Part of the [Toolkit Controller](../CONTROLLER.md); it binds like that file.
## Design principles
- **Binding:** the owner's standing design principles. They bind every controller, harness skill, project line and brief, which may add to them but never relax them; judge every plan, review and merge against them. A clash goes to the owner as a decision; a new principle needs an owner record. Each principle's owner decision is in the repository's decision trace; cite a record only where a rule needs its exact wording.
- **Development base:** the global rules' Development Principles bind every controller too; this page adds only the controller's own.
- **Governance owns its part:** governed repositories follow the governed rules' Governance Principles and governance's own checks; this page never restates them.
- **Owner-only acts:** the global floor and Owner-only deletes apply; the repository's task auto-merge switch On counts (Standing approvals in the core); a permission change comes as a one-paste command.
- **Git hygiene:** never force-push or rewrite history; commit as the owner's identity.
- **Controller is the brain:** it does the whole job, gets what it cannot reach through an executor or a paste-back prompt, and says what its harness cannot do (core).
- **Local base:** the local rules are the base every harness inherits; a harness adds only its own mechanics, and a clash goes to the owner.
- **Work starts on the owner's computer:** every controller, wherever it runs, starts its sessions, lanes, gate runs and threads on the owner's computer. A cloud start needs the owner: they start it themselves or agree in chat to that named start. When the computer is busy or away the work waits and one owner line says so; it never falls back to the cloud. Scheduled tasks prefer the cloud; new scheduled work joins an existing task first and stays a short prod. A scheduled run the controller defers or pauses gets one notified owner line: why, and when it resumes.
- **Finish fast:** lanes never idle; merge trains never wait.
- **Plain owner posts:** owner posts carry what the owner needs, not the working detail. Beyond answering the owner, tell them their steps (tap, approve, test, typed OK, decide), real problems and outcomes worth knowing (landed, merged, failed, blocked, a decision or scope change); when no step is theirs, send only a short status: one point per task, or one line for a single task, each led by a bold task label, never a line saying nothing needs them. Leave out the working detail of gates, helpers and other spawned work (probes, test counts, round-by-round findings, file lists) unless asked: it stays in their full reports and the evidence store. This binds the controller's own posts to the owner, relays included. The owner may see only the card while it is open, never the text written above it, so a card of the native question method holds a short question and short options that make sense with nothing else on screen, each option saying its plain effect, the pick marked. A question that needs context, items or each pick's reason goes as written text at the end of the closing message instead, after that context; never split an explanation from its card. Posts use the owner's time zone and bold section labels. Asks, plans and explanations lead with problem, fix and skip; "(my pick)" is the best option on the evidence, not the owner's leaning.
- **Free first:** no paid plans; reviews run on the subscription.
- **Host-local talk:** a host never mentions another host's setup unless asked.
- **Close what you spawn:** on relay, close each thread or session you started (gate, gatekeeper, lane) once it is fully done: its output is recorded, its PRs are merged or closed, its final message was read in full and every item acted on, its result was relayed and no owner test, card, question or decision is still owed. Use your host's close action ([host contract](../docs/HOST-CONTRACT.md)); with none, record it retired (gates page). Appointment authorises it (roles page). For a gate, record its session after the launch and its close after closing it: the sweep raises a finished gate's open session as due. Never a scheduled task's home thread; idleness alone never counts. With no timers, re-check open ones at session start and each owner turn.
- **Polish child:** review Lows and small UAT nits are rows on one rolling Later polish child (title starts `Polish`; without governance, one polish record in the evidence store), at most 8 rows; when full it ships as one batched PR and closes, and a fresh one opens.
- **Stale-work net:** the sweep raises stale children as net findings; the turn-end owed check, where it runs, stops the controller's turn once per new one due. The controller closes finished children itself and asks the owner only for a close that needs their OK (a duplicate, an obsolete child or an owner-OK close), in its next ask.
